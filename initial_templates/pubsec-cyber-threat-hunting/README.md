# Threat Hunting Copilot — UEBA + agentic investigation for a state SOC

**Customer (fictional):** Cascadia Security Operations Center (CSOC), protecting the Cascadia Department of Revenue (CDOR) · **Industry:** Public Sector (State & Local)

> Defensive demo. All telemetry is synthetic: public IPs are RFC 5737 documentation ranges (203.0.113.0/24, 198.51.100.0/24, 192.0.2.0/24), attacker domains use the reserved `.example` TLD, ASNs are documentation/private numbers. No real indicators, no exploit code.

## The story
CDOR (1,180 staff) holds taxpayer audit files that include **Federal Tax Information**. Its SOC sees **7,593 alerts in 90 days, 5.0% true positive**; malware and phishing are caught in hours, but **credential compromises take 15.4 days on average to detect** (370 hours, n=27) and half are found by someone outside the SOC.

**The planted attack ("low and slow").** For 19 days a password spray from 12 hosting IPs in 203.0.113.0/24 made **1,959 guesses against 212 accounts — never more than one per account per day**, so the SIEM's ">5 failures in 10 minutes" rule could never fire. Six days ago the guess for **Daniel Morales** (Senior Field Auditor) worked; after five denied/ignored MFA pushes he approved the sixth at 03:04 (MFA fatigue), a new authenticator was registered, and over five nights the attacker VPN'd in from an unmanaged PC, RDP'd into finance workstation **WS-FIN-022**, ran domain discovery, read **4,812 taxpayer audit files** on FS-TAX-01, staged a password-protected 7z archive and exfiltrated it with **58,000 DNS TXT lookups** to `update-cdnsync.example`, beaconing every ~5 minutes. The two alerts that fired (impossible travel, newly-registered domain) were closed by tier 1 as false positives.

**UEBA** (an Isolation Forest registered in Unity Catalog, `ueba_user_model@champion` / `ueba_host_model@champion`) scores every user and host daily; a day where several behaviours deviate at once scores high. This morning's queue has 7 leads: Morales (risk 99.6) and WS-FIN-022 (93.7) at the top, and five boring ones — including **Jenna Liu** (IT), signing in from a Vancouver hotel on approved travel.

## Demo script (≈12 minutes)
Dates below are those of the reference dataset (generated 2026-09-30; lead ids are assigned by risk rank, so they can shift by a position when the job is re-run on another day; "D-6" = six days before generation). Numbers come from SQL; agent wording varies run to run.

1. **Home** (`/`). Read the headline: *"For 19 days someone guessed one password per account per day. The SIEM never fired. Then a guess worked."* Tiles: **7** open UEBA leads, **2** critical, identity MTTD **15.4 days**, 0 incident cases. Point at the *UEBA risk leaderboard* (Morales 100, WS-FIN-022 94), the priority-lead swimlane, and the ATT&CK matrix (red cells = techniques with no detection rule).
2. **CISO questions.** Click the chip **"Which incident categories take us longest to detect, and how does that compare to malware and phishing?"** → dock agent calls Genie (~30 s): *credential_compromise 370.5 h (15.4 days), data_mishandling 96.5 h (4.0 days), phishing 5.0 h, malware 0.4 h.* Then **"Are we seeing a password spray? …"** → *212 accounts, 1,959 failed attempts from 12 source IPs, 2026-09-06 → 2026-09-24, max 1 failure per account per day* — a textbook low-and-slow spray. Optional: **Noisy rules** → *DNS newly-registered domain 0.4% TP (2,038 alerts), Impossible travel 0.6% (1,512)*; **FTI notification** → playbook: SEV-1, CISO within 1 h, IRS Office of Safeguards + TIGTA within 24 h (PB-1.1/1.2).
3. **Lead queue** (`/leads`). 7 leads ranked by risk, each with the behaviours that deviated and a 14-day sparkline. Morales: *5 MFA pushes denied / timed out · 153 VPN minutes from an unmanaged device*, 2 linked alerts, **1 closed as FP**.
4. **The aha — Daniel Morales** (`/leads/L-1042`, or *Investigate Daniel Morales* on Home). Show the UEBA bars jumping on 09-23 and the raw evidence (VPN from NightOwl VPS Hosting, device UNMANAGED). Click **Investigate this lead** and narrate the streamed pivots (~90–120 s, 11–13 tool calls): lead context → his sign-ins → the source range **203.0.113.** (*~1,960 attempts on 212 accounts from 12 IPs, max 1/account/day*) → VPN (*5 sessions from DESKTOP-7Q2M4KD*) → EDR for dmorales and for **WS-FIN-022** (*4,814 files touched*) → DNS for WS-FIN-022 (*update-cdnsync.example: 59,686 lookups, 58,000 TXT*) → the playbook (severity, notifications, containment).
5. **The result.** *Confirmed compromise · SEV-1 · 4,812 taxpayer records at risk.* Walk the **incident timeline** (spray 09-06 → valid password + MFA fatigue 09-23 02:47 → approval 03:04 → rogue authenticator 03:06 → VPN 03:08 → RDP to WS-FIN-022 03:17 → discovery 03:22 → OneDriveUpdater.exe 03:40 → DNS beacon 03:41 → 4,812 files 09-24/25 → 7z archive → 58,000 TXT lookups 09-26/27), each row tagged with its ATT&CK technique. The **ATT&CK mapping** shows CDOR's coverage per technique — spray (T1110.003), MFA fatigue (T1621), RDP, collection, archive and DNS exfil are *no detection*. The **containment plan** follows playbook PB-7.1: C-02 revoke sessions, C-01 disable dmorales, C-05 isolate WS-FIN-022, C-07 sinkhole the domain, C-06 block 203.0.113.0/24, C-03 remove the rogue authenticator, C-08 preserve evidence, C-04 reset, C-09 notify; **required notifications**: CISO 1 h, IRS Safeguards + TIGTA 24 h, Fusion Center 24 h.
6. **Write-back.** Keep SEV-1, click **Open incident case & request 9 containment actions** → *Incident IR-2026-0183 opened (SEV-1) · 9 containment actions requested — written to Lakebase*. Open **Incident cases** (`/cases`): the case with its containment list, ATT&CK techniques and records at risk; the lead's audit trail shows the AI investigation and the analyst decision.
7. **The benign one — Jenna Liu** (`/leads/L-1047`). Investigate (~60 s) → *Benign — explained · SEV-4*: approved travel **TR-2026-118** (Cascadia GovTech Summit, Vancouver), managed laptop LT-HQ-0088, MFA approved first push, hotel network not hosting, business hours. Click **Close as benign** → disposition + evidence written to Lakebase. (Also benign, if time: Toby Nakamura L-1044 — new service-desk hire, ticket HD-58190; Paula Whitaker L-1048 — new phone, HD-58213; Carmen Vasquez L-1046 — Q3 audit sampling, HD-58240.)
8. **Hunt analytics** (`/analytics`) — the spray in red on top of normal failures, and rule efficacy. **CISO dashboard** (`/dashboard`) — the same story for Gloria.
9. Close: **Reset the demo** (header) wipes Lakebase and re-syncs the queue from Delta.

Talking points: the lakehouse holds all the telemetry at log scale (1.7M rows here) under Unity Catalog; UEBA is a real model with a UC version and alias; every agent pivot is governed SQL you can read in the trace; the Genie agent keeps response steps and deadlines cited from the playbook; the analyst always decides; the SOC's detection gaps become a backlog (ATT&CK coverage).
