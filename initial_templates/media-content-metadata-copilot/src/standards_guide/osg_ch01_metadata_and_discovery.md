# Orbitline+ Metadata & Content Standards Guide — Chapter 1: Why metadata is discovery

**§1.1 Metadata drives every discovery surface.** On Orbitline+ a title is only as visible as its metadata. Home-screen
rows (collections) are tag-driven: a title appears in "Edge-of-Your-Seat Thrillers" only if its genres include Thriller,
in "Feel-Good Favourites" only if its moods include Feel-Good or Heartwarming. Search ranks on title, synopsis, keywords,
genres, moods, themes and talent. Recommendations ("more like this") compare tag and synopsis profiles. A title with a
generic genre, no moods and no themes is effectively invisible, however good it is.

**§1.2 The completeness score.** Every title carries a metadata completeness score from 0 to 100:
- Genre specificity — 15 points (at least one specific genre other than Drama, or two genres; 7 for Drama alone; 0 for
  generic placeholders such as "Feature Film", "General Entertainment" or "TV Series").
- Moods — 15 points for two or more (7 for one).
- Themes — 15 points for two or more (7 for one).
- Content warnings reviewed — 20 points. A title scores these points only once an editor has reviewed its content
  warnings (an explicit "no warnings" after review also counts).
- Talent — 15 points for three or more credited people (7 for one or two).
- Keywords — 10 points for five or more (5 for fewer).
- Synopsis — 10 points for 120 characters or more (4 for anything shorter).

**§1.3 Targets.** New titles must reach a completeness score of 80 before they go live. Titles below 40 are "Poor" and
must be prioritised for re-tagging. The quarterly target is 90% of the library at 80 or above.

**§1.4 Prioritisation.** Re-tag first the titles viewers finish but rarely see ("hidden gems": completion rate at or
above the genre peer median while impressions are below 40% of a well-tagged peer), then any title with a safety risk
(see Chapter 3), then everything under 40.
