# Orbitline+ Metadata & Content Standards Guide — Chapter 2: Genres, moods and themes

**§2.1 Controlled vocabulary only.** Tags must come from the Orbitline+ taxonomy (genre, mood, theme and
content-warning facets). Free-text tags are not allowed; anything outside the taxonomy is discarded at ingestion.

**§2.2 Genres.** Assign 1 to 3 genres. Never use the generic placeholders "Feature Film", "General Entertainment" or
"TV Series" as genres — they come from legacy vendor feeds and carry no discovery value. Drama may be used, but a title
tagged only "Drama" is under-specified if a more specific genre applies (Thriller, Crime, Family, Romance…). The
primary genre is the most specific one, in this precedence: Horror, Sci-Fi, Fantasy, Thriller, Mystery, Action,
Western, War, Documentary, Animation, Family, Romantic Comedy, Romance, Sports, Coming-of-Age, Comedy, Crime,
Adventure, Musical, Drama.

**§2.3 Moods.** Assign 2 to 4 moods describing how the title FEELS to watch (Tense, Atmospheric, Heartwarming,
Funny…). Moods fill the mood-driven rows ("Feel-Good Favourites", "Cozy Mysteries", "Laugh Out Loud"), so a missing
mood removes a title from those rows entirely.

**§2.4 Themes.** Assign 2 to 4 central themes (what the story is ABOUT: Smuggling, Brotherhood, Grief, First Love…).
Themes power editorial collections such as "Stormy Seas & Coastal Towns" and long-tail search.

**§2.5 Talent.** Map the director and the top-billed cast (at least three people when available) from the licensing
contract credits. Missing talent is the single most common reason a title fails talent searches.

**§2.6 AI-generated tags.** Tags proposed by the AI tagging pipeline are drafts. Each proposed content warning must
cite the scene that evidences it. An editor must review and approve the proposal before it becomes live metadata; the
approval is recorded with the editor's name and a timestamp (Chapter 6).
