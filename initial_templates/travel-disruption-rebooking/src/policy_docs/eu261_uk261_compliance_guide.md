# EU261 and UK261 — Aeralis Compliance Guide for the IROPS Desk

Document REG-EU-03 · Version 4 · Legal & Regulatory Affairs · Reflects Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 and the UK retained
version (UK261, The Air Passenger Rights and Air Travel Organisers' Licensing (Amendment) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019).
This is an internal operating summary, not legal advice.

## 1. Which Aeralis flights are covered (Article 3)
The Regulation applies to:
- passengers **departing from an airport in the EU** (UK261: in the UK), whatever the airline; and
- passengers departing from a non-EU airport to an EU airport **only if the operating airline is an EU (Community) carrier**.
Aeralis Airways is a **US carrier**. Therefore:
- **AE 97 Frankfurt (FRA) → Chicago (ORD)** is covered by **EU261** (departs the EU).
- **AE 99 London Heathrow (LHR) → Chicago (ORD)** is covered by **UK261** (departs the UK).
- **AE 96 Chicago (ORD) → Frankfurt** and **AE 98 Chicago → London** are **NOT covered** by EU261 or UK261: they depart a
  US airport on a non-EU / non-UK carrier. US DOT rules and Aeralis commitments apply instead — even when the passenger is
  an EU citizen.

## 2. Cancellation — what the passenger is always owed (Articles 5, 8, 9)
When a covered flight is cancelled, Aeralis must offer:
- **Article 8 — the choice** between (a) a refund of the full ticket price within 7 days (plus a return flight to the first
  point of departure where relevant), (b) **re-routing under comparable transport conditions to the final destination at the
  earliest opportunity**, or (c) re-routing at a later date of the passenger's choosing. The European Commission's
  interpretative guidelines (2016) and CJEU case law treat "earliest opportunity" as including **flights of other carriers**
  when the airline's own flights would arrive significantly later. Aeralis therefore uses Lumen Air partner seats for covered
  passengers without applying the domestic cost caps (policy OPS-IROPS-01 §4.3).
- **Article 9 — care, free of charge**: meals and refreshments in reasonable relation to the waiting time; **hotel
  accommodation where a stay of one or more nights becomes necessary**; transport between the airport and the hotel; and
  two telephone calls, emails or messages.
- **The duty of care applies even when the cancellation is caused by extraordinary circumstances.** The CJEU confirmed this
  in McDonagh v Ryanair (C-12/11, 2013), where the cause was the volcanic ash cloud.

## 3. Compensation (Article 7) and extraordinary circumstances (Article 5(3))
- Compensation is **EUR 250 / 400 / 600** by distance (UK261: GBP 220 / 350 / 520). FRA–ORD and LHR–ORD are more than
  3,500 km, so the amount would be **EUR 600 / GBP 520** per passenger.
- **No compensation is owed when the cancellation is caused by extraordinary circumstances that could not have been avoided
  even if all reasonable measures had been taken** (Article 5(3)). Recital 14 lists "meteorological conditions
  incompatible with the operation of the flight".
- Winter Storm Harlan: AE 97 and AE 99 were cancelled because Chicago O'Hare was closed to arrivals by the storm (ground
  stop) and the aircraft could not operate the rotation. The desk records the cause as **extraordinary circumstances
  (weather)**: **compensation is not owed, but re-routing (Art. 8) and full care including hotels (Art. 9) are owed to every
  passenger.** Claims are handled by Customer Relations with the METAR / ground-stop evidence pack.
- Compensation may also be excluded when the passenger is informed at least 14 days in advance, or re-routed within the
  Article 5(1)(c) time windows. None applies on the day of the storm.

## 4. What to tell covered passengers
The notification must include: the reason for cancellation, the re-routing offered, the right to choose a refund instead,
the hotel, meal and transport care provided, and a link to Aeralis's EU261 information notice (Article 14 requires a
written notice setting out the rules for compensation and assistance).
