# US DOT Passenger Rights — Refunds, Cancellations and Accessibility (Aeralis Desk Summary)

Document REG-US-04 · Legal & Regulatory Affairs · Internal operating summary, not legal advice.

## 1. Automatic refunds (14 CFR Part 260, DOT final rule of April 2024, in force since 28 October 2024)
- When an airline **cancels a flight or makes a significant change** and the passenger **does not accept** the alternative
  transportation or travel credit offered (or no alternative is offered), the passenger is entitled to an **automatic cash
  refund** of the ticket price, including taxes and fees and any optional services paid for but not used.
- The refund must go to the **original form of payment**: within **7 business days** for credit-card purchases and
  **20 calendar days** for other forms of payment. A voucher or credit is only allowed if the passenger chooses it.
- A **significant change** includes a departure or arrival moved by **3 hours or more for a domestic flight** or **6 hours
  or more for an international flight**, a change of departure or arrival airport, additional connections, a downgrade to a
  lower class of service, or a connection at a less accessible airport for a passenger with a disability.
- This applies to every fare, **including Basic Economy**, and whatever the cause of the cancellation (weather included).
- Airlines must tell passengers about the right to a refund when they notify them of the cancellation or change.

## 2. Downgrades
If a passenger is involuntarily moved to a lower cabin (for example First to Economy on a re-accommodation), the airline
must refund the fare difference.

## 3. Hotels and meals under US rules
US federal law does **not** require airlines to provide hotels or meals during a cancellation. Each airline's customer-
service commitments (shown on DOT's Airline Customer Service Dashboard) apply only to **controllable** delays and
cancellations. For Aeralis, see CARE-02 (weather = uncontrollable).

## 4. Passengers with disabilities (Air Carrier Access Act, 14 CFR Part 382)
- Airlines must provide wheelchair and escort assistance for enplaning, deplaning and connecting, and may not refuse
  transportation on the basis of disability.
- During irregular operations the airline must give passengers with disabilities the same information and rebooking
  options as other passengers, in an accessible format, and continue to provide the requested assistance.
- Aeralis policy goes further: special-assistance passengers are re-accommodated first and, if an overnight is unavoidable,
  receive an accessible hotel room and transport (CARE-02 §3.2).

## 5. Tarmac delays and unaccompanied minors
Tarmac-delay limits (14 CFR 259.4) do not apply to cancelled flights at the gate. There is no federal UMNR regulation; the
airline's own UMNR policy applies (SSR-04).
