# Corvane Supply Planning Policy — Chapter 4: Inter-Plant Transfers and Excess & Obsolete Inventory

Corvane policy CSP-04. Owner: Director of Supply Planning and Plant Controllers.

## 4.1 Use Corvane stock before buying
Before buying or expediting, planners must check whether another Corvane plant holds usable stock of the same component above its own safety stock for the full 13-week horizon. **Excess and obsolete (E&O) stock must be offered first.** Components are interchangeable across plants when the part number is identical (for example HX-220 gear housings built for the sunset SG-300 at Monterrey are the same part used by the SG-250/400/600 at Dayton).

## 4.2 Transfer lanes and cost
- Monterrey to Dayton: bonded truck under USMCA, 5 days door to door including customs, USD 2,900 per truck (up to 800 housings per truck) plus USD 2.40 per unit handling and customs.
- Transatlantic transfers (Dayton or Monterrey to Wroclaw and back): air freight, 5 to 6 days, per the transfer-lane table.
A transfer is booked as an inter-plant transfer order (TO) by the receiving plant's planner; the shipping plant may not refuse E&O stock.

## 4.3 Excess and obsolete definitions
- **Obsolete**: no forward requirement at the plant in the 13-week horizon and no issue for more than 90 days (for example parts of a sunset product).
- **Excess**: more than 26 weeks of cover; the units above 26 weeks are excess.
E&O is reserved at 100% of standard cost for obsolete stock and 50% for excess stock. Transferring E&O stock to a plant that consumes it **releases the reserve** — the value comes back to the P&L — so transfers of E&O stock are always preferred over new purchases.

## 4.4 Keep what the shipping plant needs
Never transfer below the shipping plant's own safety stock. For obsolete stock the shipping plant's safety stock is zero.
