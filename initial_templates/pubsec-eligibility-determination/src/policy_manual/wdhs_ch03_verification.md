# Westbrook Department of Human Services — Economic Assistance Policy Manual
## Chapter 3 — Verification
Revision 2026-1.

### §3.1 Identity (SNAP)
Verify the identity of the head of household (and any authorized representative) before certification (7 CFR 273.2(f)(1)(vii)). Acceptable documents: a current, unexpired Westbrook driver license or state ID card, a U.S. passport, a tribal ID, a work or school ID with photo, or a birth certificate. **An expired ID is not acceptable as the sole proof of identity.** A collateral contact (for example, a landlord or employer who knows the applicant) may be used instead. Identity is the only verification required before expedited benefits are issued.

### §3.2 Residency
The household must live in Westbrook (7 CFR 273.3). Verify residency with a lease or rental agreement, rent receipt, mortgage statement, or a utility bill dated within the last 60 days that shows a Westbrook service address. A document showing an address in another state does not verify Westbrook residency; ask the household to explain and to provide current proof. Homeless households are not required to have a fixed address — accept the household's statement.

### §3.3 Earned income
Gross non-exempt income must be verified before certification (7 CFR 273.2(f)(1)(i)).
- **Pay stubs** must cover the 30 days immediately before the filing date (for example, two biweekly or four weekly stubs). A pay stub dated more than 30 days before the filing date is **stale**: it may not be used alone to establish current income. Request current stubs or an employer statement (DHS-1152).
- **Terminated employment**: verify with a layoff or termination notice, a letter from the employer, or a collateral contact, showing the last day worked and the date of the final paycheck. Once verified, income from the terminated job is **not** counted as anticipated income for the months after the final paycheck (see §4.3). Pay stubs from before a layoff document past income only.
- **No income**: accept a signed Statement of No Income (DHS-2107) unless the statement is questionable.

### §3.4 Requesting missing verification (DHS-1150)
When verification is missing, send one written Request for Verification (DHS-1150) that lists **every** missing item, in plain language, with examples of acceptable documents, the due date, and how to submit (upload, mail, fax, drop-off). Give the household **at least 10 calendar days** to respond (7 CFR 273.2(c)(5), 273.2(f)). Send the request in the household's preferred language. Requesting items one at a time is not permitted — each extra round trip adds weeks and is the leading cause of late decisions.

### §3.5 Duty to assist
The caseworker must help the household obtain verification when the household has difficulty (7 CFR 273.2(f)(5)). Examples: calling the employer with the household's consent, using state wage records, or accepting a collateral contact. Rural households without broadband or transportation must be offered help before a procedural denial is considered.

### §3.6 Discrepancies
When a document conflicts with the application (different name, address, or income), resolve the discrepancy before certification. Minor name variations (middle initial, hyphenated surname) are not discrepancies.
