# Westbrook Department of Human Services — Economic Assistance Policy Manual
## Chapter 2 — Application Processing and Timeliness (SNAP and Medicaid)
Revision 2026-1. Applies to all county offices and the State Surge Team.

### §2.1 Filing date
The filing date is the day the office receives an application containing at least the applicant's name, address and signature, by any channel: online portal, mail, fax, phone (recorded signature) or in person. The processing clock starts on the filing date, not on the date a caseworker first opens the case. (7 CFR 273.2(c)(1))

### §2.2 The 30-day SNAP processing standard
The office must provide an eligible household an opportunity to participate, or send a notice of denial, **no later than 30 calendar days after the filing date**. (7 CFR 273.2(g)(1)) County offices are measured monthly on the share of SNAP applications decided within 30 days. The federal performance expectation is 95% timely; USDA Food and Nutrition Service may require a corrective action plan when a state falls below it.

### §2.3 Expedited service (7-day standard)
Screen every SNAP application for expedited service on the filing date. A household is entitled to expedited service when ANY of the following is true (7 CFR 273.2(i)(1)):
1. gross monthly income is less than $150 AND liquid resources are $100 or less; or
2. combined gross monthly income and liquid resources are less than the household's monthly rent/mortgage plus utilities; or
3. the household is a destitute migrant or seasonal farmworker household with liquid resources of $100 or less.

Expedited households must receive benefits **no later than the 7th calendar day after the filing date**. (7 CFR 273.2(i)(3)) Only identity must be verified before issuing expedited benefits; all other verification may be postponed. A household that recently lost its job and has no current income usually meets criterion 1 even if prior pay stubs show high wages. Missing the 7-day deadline is a serious timeliness error; document the reason in the case record.

### §2.4 Interview
Every SNAP household must have an interview before certification (7 CFR 273.2(e)). Offer a telephone interview by default. Schedule the interview within 10 days of the filing date. If the household misses the interview, send a Notice of Missed Interview (DHS-1144) — do not deny before day 30.

### §2.5 Medicaid timeliness
Medicaid applications (non-disability) must be decided within **45 days** of the filing date (42 CFR 435.912(c)(3)). Applications based on disability: 90 days.

### §2.6 Agency-caused delays
If an application is not decided by day 30 because of the office's own delay (for example, the verification request was sent late, or no interview was scheduled), the household **must not** be denied for failure to provide verification. The case stays open and, if eligible, benefits are restored back to the filing month. (7 CFR 273.2(h)(3)) A procedural denial is only valid when the household was given a written request listing every missing item and at least 10 days to respond.

### §2.7 Workload balancing and the State Surge Team
When a county office's open caseload exceeds 40 cases per eligibility worker, or its monthly 30-day timeliness drops below 80% for two consecutive months, the Division of Economic Assistance may assign the State Surge Team. Surge caseworkers work the county's oldest cases first, then expedited cases, then new applications.
