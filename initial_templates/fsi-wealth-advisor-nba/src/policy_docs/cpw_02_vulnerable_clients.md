# Calder Pine Private Wealth — Vulnerable Client & Bereavement Policy
Revision 2026-1. Owner: Office of the Chief Compliance Officer with the Client Care team.

## §1 Who is a vulnerable client
§1.1 A client is **vulnerable** when any of the following applies: (a) the client is a **specified adult** — age 80 or older, or age 18+ with a mental or physical impairment that renders the individual unable to protect his or her own interests (FINRA Rule 2165); (b) the client has experienced the **death of a spouse or partner in the last 12 months**; (c) the client is going through a divorce; (d) the advisor observes signs of diminished capacity or undue influence.
§1.2 Grief affects decision-making. The first months after a bereavement are when clients are most likely to make hurried, irreversible decisions — and when an heir or third party is most likely to steer the money.

## §2 What changes for a vulnerable client
§2.1 **No Complex products** (structured notes, private credit, alternatives, SBLOC) for 12 months after a bereavement, or at any time for a specified adult, without Chief Compliance Officer approval.
§2.2 **Moderate-complexity products** (managed portfolios, direct indexing, rollovers) only after a **documented planning meeting** has taken place and a supervisor has pre-approved the recommendation.
§2.3 Simple actions that preserve flexibility are preferred: keep an emergency reserve liquid, ladder maturing CDs or Treasuries, move idle cash to a government money market fund, and review beneficiaries, titling and the estate plan.
§2.4 Never pressure a timeline. The outreach message must be empathetic and invitational — no product names, no rates promised, no urgency language beyond factual dates (e.g. a CD maturity date).

## §3 The bereavement playbook (0-12 months)
§3.1 **Within 30 days:** personal call from the advisor (not an email); offer the estate-settlement checklist; confirm the trusted contact person.
§3.2 **First planning meeting (30-120 days):** beneficiary designations, re-titling of joint and transfer-on-death accounts, spousal IRA rollover options, cash-flow needs for the next 12 months, and any maturing instruments. Invite the trusted contact or the client's chosen family member if the client wishes.
§3.3 **After the plan is documented:** long-term investment of inherited or insurance proceeds, within the suitability rules.
§3.4 Data from 2025-26: households whose advisor called within 30 days of a bereavement or inheritance kept about 95% of their assets over the next six months; households nobody called lost roughly 40% in transfers to heirs' advisors and online banks.

## §4 Trusted contact and temporary holds
§4.1 Advisors must make a reasonable effort to obtain the name of a **trusted contact person** for every account (FINRA Rule 4512). The trusted contact may be contacted about suspected exploitation, the client's health status, or the identity of a legal guardian, executor or trustee — the trusted contact has no authority to trade.
§4.2 If the advisor reasonably believes a specified adult is being financially exploited, the firm may place a **temporary hold** on a disbursement for up to 15 business days, extendable by 10 business days after internal review (FINRA Rule 2165). Escalate to Compliance the same day.
§4.3 A family member "helping with the finances" is not an authorized party unless a power of attorney is on file. Share account information only with the client or an authorized party.
