# Calder Pine Private Wealth — Lending & Rollover Playbooks for Advisors
Revision 2026-2.

## §1 Mortgage refinance window
§1.1 A client is in the **refi window** when the mortgage rate is at least **0.75 percentage points above** the current 30-year fixed rate (5.90% on September 28, 2026) and the balance is at least $150,000.
§1.2 Estimate the monthly saving with the standard amortization formula on the remaining balance over 30 years. Example: $780,000 at 7.25% ($5,321/month principal and interest) refinanced at 5.90% ($4,626/month) saves about **$695/month**; with ~2% closing costs (~$15,600) the break-even is about 23 months.
§1.3 Refer to a Calder Pine Bank mortgage officer; deliver D-REFI. A refinance is a Simple action for suitability purposes.

## §2 Liquidity without selling: SBLOC
§2.1 For a known cash need (home purchase, tax bill, business) an SBLOC can avoid selling appreciated securities and realizing capital gains. It is a **Complex** product: requires Extensive experience, risk score ≥ 3, and a Low liquidity need is not required.
§2.2 Advance rates: 70% of diversified equities, 50% if one position exceeds 30% of the pledged account (concentration), 0% for restricted stock in a blackout window. Stress-test a 30% market decline before recommending.
§2.3 Alternatives to present: a bridge from the refinance (cash-out), selling diversified holdings first, a HELOC.

## §3 401(k) rollovers after retirement or a job change
§3.1 A rollover recommendation is a Reg BI recommendation and, for advisory accounts, a fiduciary one under DOL PTE 2020-02. Deliver D-ROLL (plan vs IRA comparison) before the client decides — this makes the recommendation **REVIEW** status until the comparison is documented (rule S7).
§3.2 Money can stay in the plan; there is no deadline to roll, except that a lump-sum check must be deposited within 60 days. Prefer a direct trustee-to-trustee rollover.
§3.3 A retiree's first meeting covers income needs, Social Security timing and the withdrawal order before any investment recommendation.

## §4 Maturing CDs and idle cash after the 2026 rate cuts
§4.1 The Fed cut rates three times (June, July, September 2026): Fed funds 4.50% → 3.75%. A 12-month CD bought at 5.10% renews at 3.55%; the bank sweep pays 0.25%; government money market funds yield ~3.60%.
§4.2 For a maturing CD, present at least: renew, a Treasury/CD ladder (keeps FDIC or Treasury backing and staggers maturities), a government MMF for the reserve, and — only if suitable and not for a vulnerable client within 12 months — a managed portfolio for long-term money.
§4.3 Contact the client at least 10 days before maturity; an un-actioned CD rolls into the sweep at 0.25% and is the most common source of cash leaving the firm for online banks.
