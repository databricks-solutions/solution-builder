# Calder Pine Private Wealth — Best Interest & Suitability Policy
Revision 2026-2 (effective July 1, 2026). Owner: Office of the Chief Compliance Officer. Applies to every recommendation made by a Calder Pine wealth advisor to a retail client (brokerage and advisory accounts).

## §1 Scope and standard of conduct
§1.1 Every recommendation of a security, an investment strategy, an account type, a rollover or a loan secured by securities must be in the client's **best interest** at the time it is made, without placing the firm's or the advisor's interest ahead of the client's (SEC Regulation Best Interest, 17 CFR 240.15l-1; Investment Advisers Act fiduciary duty for advisory accounts).
§1.2 Reg BI has four component obligations: **Disclosure, Care, Conflict of Interest, Compliance**. This policy implements the Care Obligation (17 CFR 240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii)): the advisor must understand the product's risks, rewards and costs, have a reasonable basis to believe it is in the best interest of THIS client based on the client's investment profile, and consider reasonably available alternatives.
§1.3 A model score (for example a next-best-action propensity score) is **never** a reasonable basis on its own. Propensity measures how likely a client is to accept an offer, not whether the offer is suitable. Every model-ranked action must pass the suitability rules below before it can be presented to the client.

## §2 Client investment profile and the suitability rules
The investment profile (risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, investment experience, financial situation, age, other holdings) must be updated at least every 36 months and after any life event.

§2.1 **Risk tolerance (S1).** Risk tolerance is scored 1-5: Conservative (1), Moderately Conservative (2), Moderate (3), Growth (4), Aggressive (5). A product may be recommended only if the client's risk score is at or above the product's minimum risk score in the Product Suitability Matrix (§4).
§2.2 **Time horizon (S2).** The client's time horizon must be at least the product's minimum holding period. Buffered structured notes and private credit funds require at least 5 years; managed portfolios 3 years.
§2.3 **Liquidity (S3).** Illiquid products (structured notes held to maturity, interval funds with quarterly 5% redemption limits, lock-ups) may be recommended only to clients with a **Low** liquidity need. A client with a Medium or High liquidity need fails S3.
§2.4 **Knowledge & experience (S4).** See the Complex Products Policy §3.1: Complex products require **Extensive** investment experience.
§2.5 **Concentration (S6).** A single Complex product may not exceed **10% of the client's estimated liquid net worth**. For a securities-based line of credit, collateral where one position exceeds 30% of the pledged account requires a reduced advance rate and supervisor review.

## §3 Complex products
§3.1 Complex products are: buffered or autocallable **structured notes**, **private credit / interval funds** and other alternatives, leveraged or inverse ETFs, and **securities-based lines of credit (SBLOC)**. They require Extensive investment experience, a documented explanation of how the product works in the client file, and supervisor approval (FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-08, complex products and options).
§3.2 Complex products may never be recommended to a vulnerable client (see the Vulnerable Client Policy).
§3.3 **Lessons from 2025-26 complaints.** 121 of the 148 written complaints received in the last 18 months (82%) were on complex products sold to clients who would fail today's rules — most often buffered structured notes sold to recently bereaved or conservative clients who had just received an inheritance. Inheritance proceeds are "money in motion", not risk capacity.

## §5 Documentation
Every recommendation must record: the client profile used, the rule results, the alternatives considered, the disclosures delivered (§ Product Disclosures) and the client's decision. The Advisor Morning Book records this automatically when the advisor logs a decision.
