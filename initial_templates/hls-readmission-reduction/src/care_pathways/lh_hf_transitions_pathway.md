# Larkspur Health — Heart Failure Care Transitions Pathway (CTP-HF-03, rev. 4)

Owner: Care Transitions & Quality · Applies to: every inpatient discharged with a principal diagnosis of heart failure (HRRP HF cohort and all payers) at Larkspur Regional Medical Center, Millbrook Community Hospital and Castle Ridge Hospital.

## 1. Why this pathway exists
Heart failure is Larkspur's highest-volume HRRP condition and its largest readmission cost driver. Patients seen by a clinician within 7 days of discharge are readmitted markedly less often than patients with no early visit; the first 7 days after discharge are when volume status and new medications destabilize.

## 2. Before the patient leaves (non-negotiable elements)
2.1 **7-day follow-up appointment booked before discharge.** The discharging unit books a visit with the HF Transitions Clinic (preferred) or cardiology/PCP **dated within 7 days of discharge** and records the date in the After-Visit Summary. "Patient to call to schedule" does **not** meet this standard.
2.2 **Medication reconciliation** by a pharmacist or the discharge nurse, reconciling home medications against the discharge list. Any change in loop diuretic dose, a new RAAS agent (ARNI/ACEi/ARB), a new mineralocorticoid antagonist (spironolactone/eplerenone) or an SGLT2 inhibitor is flagged as a **high-risk medication change**.
2.3 **Labs after a high-risk medication change**: a basic metabolic panel (potassium, creatinine) **within 3-5 days** when a loop diuretic dose was increased or an ARNI/MRA was started. The order is placed before discharge, with a draw location the patient can reach.
2.4 **Teach-back** on daily weights, 2 g sodium diet, fluid guidance and red flags: weight gain > 2 lb in a day or > 5 lb in a week, worsening breathlessness, swelling.
2.5 **Scale check**: confirm the patient has a working scale at home; if not, one is issued from the unit supply.

## 3. Risk-based intensity (uses the readmission risk score shown in the Care Transitions worklist)
| Risk tier | Additional elements |
|---|---|
| High (≥ 30%) | 48-hour post-discharge phone call by an RN navigator; home health referral when the patient lives alone, has EF ≤ 40% or had ≥ 2 ED visits in 6 months; pharmacist phone follow-up at 72 hours; consider remote weight monitoring |
| Medium (18-29%) | 48-72-hour phone call; home health if lives alone or new diuretic dose |
| Low (< 18%) | Standard follow-up visit and teach-back |

## 4. Social needs
- **Transportation barrier** (does not drive, no reliable ride): book a Larkspur Ride Assist trip for the follow-up visit and the lab draw, or convert the first visit to a home visit / telehealth plus a home-health lab draw.
- **Medication cost concern**: refer to the outpatient pharmacy financial counselor before discharge; use meds-to-beds where available.
- **Lives alone**: confirm who will check on the patient in the first 72 hours; strongly consider home health.

## 5. Home health referral criteria (HF)
Refer to Larkspur Home Health for skilled nursing when ANY of: lives alone with ≥ 1 high-risk medication change; EF ≤ 40% with a diuretic dose change; ≥ 2 HF admissions or ED visits in the last 6 months; unable to perform daily weights. First visit within 48 hours of discharge.

## 6. Documentation
The transition plan (appointment date, med rec status, labs ordered, home health, transport) is documented in the Care Transitions worklist and becomes the outreach task list for the navigator team.
