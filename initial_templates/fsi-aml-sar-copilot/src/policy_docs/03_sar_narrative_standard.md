# SAR Narrative Writing Standard (Procedure SAR-04)

Fictional internal standard for Northwind Federal Bank, aligned with FinCEN's "Guidance on Preparing a Complete & Sufficient SAR Narrative".

## Principles
- The narrative (FinCEN SAR Part V) is the **most important section**: law enforcement must understand the activity without the supporting file.
- Be **chronological, factual, concise**. No speculation beyond what the facts support; say "appears", "is consistent with".
- Answer the **five Ws and how**: *Who* is conducting the activity; *What* instruments/mechanisms; *When* (date range); *Where* (branches, accounts, jurisdictions); *Why* it is suspicious; *How* the scheme operates.

## Required structure
1. **Introduction** — purpose of the filing, subject(s), typology suspected, total amount and date range, SAR type (initial / continuing).
2. **Subject information** — names, account numbers, relationship to each other (shared address, phone, device), occupation/business stated at onboarding.
3. **Chronology of activity** — cash deposits (count, range, total, branches), transfers between subjects, wires out (dates, amounts, beneficiaries, countries).
4. **Red flags / why suspicious** — reference the specific red flags (e.g. RF-03 #1, #2, #3, #6, #8).
5. **Bank actions** — alerts reviewed, prior alerts and closures, account restrictions, escalation, law-enforcement contact if any.
6. **Conclusion** — summary sentence and statement that supporting documentation is retained and available on request.

## Style rules
- Amounts in USD with commas ($9,450.00). Dates as MM/DD/YYYY.
- Use subject names consistently; define an abbreviation once (e.g. "Bayline").
- Do not include the rules or model score; describe the behaviour instead.
- Maximum ~1,500 words for a multi-subject network case.
