# Structuring & CTR-Evasion Red-Flag Guide (Investigator Reference RF-03)

Fictional internal guidance for Northwind Federal Bank, based on public FinCEN and FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual material.

## Legal basis
- A Currency Transaction Report (CTR) is required for cash transactions **over $10,000** in a business day (31 CFR 1010.311), including multiple transactions aggregated for the same person.
- **Structuring** — breaking up cash transactions to evade the CTR requirement — is a federal crime under **31 U.S.C. 5324**, regardless of whether the underlying funds are legitimate.

## Red flags (score each one found in the case)
1. **Repeated cash deposits just under $10,000** (typically $8,000–$9,999) on consecutive or near-consecutive days.
2. The same customer, or several **linked customers, depositing at multiple branches** on the same or adjacent days.
3. Deposits by **third parties / money mules** who have no apparent connection to the business, followed by transfer to a common beneficiary.
4. Cash activity **far above the expected profile** declared at onboarding (occupation, expected monthly cash).
5. **Newly opened accounts** (under 12 months) with sudden high cash volume.
6. **Rapid movement**: cash deposited and moved out (internal transfer, P2P, wire) within 1–3 days, leaving low balances.
7. Customer asks about CTR thresholds, or reduces a deposit after being told a CTR will be filed.
8. Shared identifiers across unrelated customers: **same address/suite, phone number or online-banking device**.

## Typology: funnel account
Cash is deposited into accounts in one geography by multiple people and withdrawn or wired elsewhere shortly after (FinCEN Advisory FIN-2014-A005). When the collecting account is a business, check whether stated business (e.g. vehicle export) is consistent with cash receipts.

## What does NOT alone justify a SAR
- A cash-intensive business (restaurant, car wash, laundromat) with deposits consistent with its historical pattern, one home branch and CTRs already filed for >$10K days. Document the comparison to peer and history, then close.
